Voice & tone · v0

A family farm talking to its neighbors.

Warm, plainspoken, lightly funny, and scrupulously honest. This is descriptive, not prescriptive — drawn from 28 versions of the homepage update copy (May 2024 – Feb 2026); every example below is verbatim from a past update. It's the seed the enforceable guide (and eventual in-app content checks) grows from.

How this applies in the dashboard
This guide is the brand voice — for customer-facing copy (farm updates, product descriptions, market posts). The dashboard's own microcopy (buttons, labels, empty states, errors) follows the product-UI voice in Principles 9: active voice, sentence case, plain verbs, name-by-what-the-operator-controls. Where they meet, the brand mechanics carry over — smart quotes, the value-prop terms spelled right (pasture-raised, non-GMO), and the same warm, honest register. Don't put the employee gag on a Save button.

The voice in one line

A small family farm talking to its neighbors: warm, plainspoken, lightly funny, and scrupulously honest.

Core traits

1 · "We," sometimes "I" — a real family, not a brand

The default is we / our, slipping into first-person singular for personal asides, and that's part of the charm.

"I'm sure I say it every year, but I can't believe fall is here already."

2 · The flock are "employees" — the signature running joke

Hens are personified as staff across years of updates. Keep it alive.

"We'll soon be heading to Pennsylvania to pick up 100 new employees."
"Until then, we only pay them minimum chicken wage."
"Our new help is on the farm settling in."

3 · Dry, understated humor — never zany

The wit rides inside otherwise practical sentences.

"We may be wearing long johns, but the Foster Farmer's Market will be hanging on until October 26th."
"They taste just as good when there's snow on the ground!"

4 · Teach, don't preach

Updates explain the why — molting, pasture rotation, ready-to-lay pullets — often with a parenthetical or em-dash definition.

"our chickens are molting — the time of year when hens slow or stop egg production and focus their energy on replacing feathers."

5 · Skeptical quotes around industry labels

Marketing-speak they distrust gets visibly held at arm's length.

eggs called "free-range", eggs called "cage-free"… chicken before it became a "manufactured" product. Pasture-raised chicken tastes like chicken!

6 · Radical honesty about the hard stuff

Bad news is named plainly, explained, and — where warranted — apologized for. Prices come with exact figures and rationale.

"Sadly, we have recently lost several hens to predation by the local hawk population."
"Our price will increase 17 cents per egg to $7.00 per dozen, beginning February 8th. This modest price increase will allow us to provide the highest quality product we can."

7 · Gratitude, every time — and it closes the update

Nearly every post thanks customers, often nudging them to spread the word.

"Thank you, as always, for supporting North Foster Farm and all of our local farmer friends."

Signature structural moves

  • Seasonal titles. Every update is named by season or month — Spring Update, Mid-Fall Update, June Update.
  • Bold lead-in labels for multi-item news: New employees. … Farmers markets. … Non-GMO. …
  • Event blocks use a fixed shape — When: / Time: / Where: with the full address.
  • Concrete specifics build trust — exact dates, addresses, flock counts ("100 new hens"), even dimensions ("16′ × 50′ high-tunnel greenhouse").
  • The standing footer appears on every update: schedule changes are posted to the site, plus the email-list signup line.
  • The evergreen "We love eggs." block has run unchanged since the first commit — the canonical brand-story section, not rewritten per update.

Mechanics

PersonFirst-person plural default; singular allowed for warmth.
PunctuationEm-dashes for asides/definitions; ellipses for the occasional comic beat. Smart/curly quotes and apostrophes throughout.
RhythmComplete, flowing, conversational sentences in paragraphs of 3–5. A newsletter from a person, not clipped ad copy.
Value-prop drumbeatpasture-raised, non-GMO, moved to fresh grass daily, never any hormones or antibiotics, humane. "Pasture-raised" stays hyphenated; "non-GMO" keeps that casing.
ClosingEnd warm — thanks, and an invitation to come by or spread the word.

This, not that

Off-voice (too "brand")
  • "Eggs and meat, the way they're supposed to taste."
  • "Come find us."
  • "New arrivals."
  • Clipped one-line fragments for punch.
On-voice (North Foster Farm)
  • "Pasture-raised chicken tastes like chicken!"
  • "We hope to see you there!" / "Stop by our booth…"
  • "Our laying flock will be welcoming another group of new recruits."
  • Full, conversational sentences that explain and invite.

For the eventual content checker

When in-app checks become real, the traits split into two buckets — this is the spec for what to automate versus surface for a human pass.

Mechanically enforceable (lintable) Editorial (human judgment)
Smart quotes required (no straight quotes); "pasture-raised" hyphenation; "non-GMO" casing; seasonal-title pattern present; standing footer present; a gratitude line near the close. The employee gag; the dry humor; teach-don't-preach explanations; the honesty about hard news. Prompt for these and flag if absent — not mechanically verifiable.
